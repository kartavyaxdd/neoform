# Accepted-change reviews

The preview, Progression sheet and program review contain a collapsed **Accepted changes** section. It derives reviews from existing decisions and completed observations; it writes no competing score or reward ledger. History, imports and v1–v3 backups keep their existing format. No database migration or provider is required.

## Conservative original app policies

- **Keep change:** the two latest eligible follow-ups meet the exact accepted row reps and intended reserve. This is stricter than the older outcome ledger's ±1-RIR descriptive label. It describes target attainment, not measured growth or proof of safety.
- **Review reversal:** two comparable follow-ups miss accepted reps or intended reserve, even if they remain above the exercise's broad rep-range floor.
- **Observe longer:** fewer than two eligible attempts, mixed results or an unconfirmed latest attempt. Missing effort, incomplete/skipped work and changed execution cannot become successes.
- **Reassess context:** applicable pain/recovery flags, changed plan/setup/method/order/phase, superseded targets or a last eligible follow-up older than 21 days. There is no automatic restoration of a stale experiment.

These are conservative software review rules informed by TRAINING-PRINCIPLES.md, not validated biological thresholds or claims that a change caused improvement. User edits and coordinated bundles remain explicitly labelled; their results do not establish algorithm success or each component's causal contribution. Session feeling, pump and soreness are not growth measurements.

## Evidence and application

Each completed session contributes at most once per experiment. The latest saved correction must still match its actual observations, prescriptions and execution confirmation. Additional independent checks cover phase, exercise identity, equipment/setup, method, full intended dose and preceding exercise order. Future sessions and future confirmations are excluded. One-off choices are not assigned invented independent follow-ups. Added-bodyweight comparisons need a known, timely and materially stable reported bodyweight reference.

An accepted future target is not escalated again after a single success. Two qualifying follow-ups must support keeping it first. Repeated misses can offer the exact previously previewed rows through the existing explicit scope/acceptance flow. Eligible restorations are limited to still-current, isolated, unedited rep/load/set additions whose prior rows reduce the reviewed demand; load reductions require available supported inventory. Reductions do not reverse into increases after poor results. Rep-range, complex, edited, bundled, stale or interveningly changed experiments require manual review. Current prescribed targets must exactly match the recorded accepted target before a restoration is available. Merely clearing row overrides would incorrectly re-prefill a failed heavier load, so restoration explicitly assigns reviewed prior rows.

Two accepted one-workout increases that both miss also block further suggested increases in that context. Two new comparable exposures meeting the previously proposed rows and reserve clear that conservative guard. This changes the response to observed failure; it does not raise progression caps or automatically increase volume.

For volume experiments, performance attainment alone cannot justify Keep or another increase: require distinct, fresh readiness observations after both assessed exposures, within seven days, reporting usual context, low systemic fatigue, pain-free recovery and low applicable muscle soreness. General and muscle timestamps are checked separately. Reports cannot be reused for both exposures. Existing priority, diet, time, weekly set and progression caps remain.

Restoration is a proposal. Opening a review changes nothing. Preview choices are ephemeral until Start; today/future scope, stale-evidence rejection, immutable completed history and durable saving remain governed by PREVIEW-GUIDANCE.md. The original ledger remains visible and old outcome classifications are not rewritten.
